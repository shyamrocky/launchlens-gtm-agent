# 📰 Overview

Develop an evidence-led introduction to Cherry Float that makes the flavor concept clear and gives adult flavor explorers a reason to learn more. The opportunity is a planning hypothesis, not established consumer demand. Product understanding and consistent descriptions come before any claim that a market can buy the product.

## Product summary

- **Category and flavor:** flavored Coca-Cola soda with cherry and vanilla notes (PROD-001, PROD-002). Communication value: explain the flavor combination in plain language, without making sensory-test or ingredient-content claims.
- **Inspiration:** classic soda floats (PROD-003). Communication value: use a familiar flavor reference without implying dairy content or a health benefit.
- **Variants:** original/full-sugar and zero-sugar variants are documented in source-specific markets (PROD-004). Communication value: distinguish variants accurately. Selected-market variant/package eligibility: TBD — Pending Launch Calendar / Readiness Tracker.

## Launch strategy

- **Campaign approach:** develop a coordinated flavor-discovery story centered on classic soda-float inspiration and Coca-Cola identity (PROD-001, PROD-002, PROD-003). Use the three Key Launch Messages consistently across drafts. Avoid price promotions or availability-led calls to action while market eligibility is unresolved.
- **LinkedIn:** use a concise product-story draft explaining the flavor concept and why the team chose this positioning hypothesis. Keep it professional and accessible; no invented company quotes, partnerships or performance results. Example tactic: a short concept introduction with the supporting product references, held from release until eligible.

- **Blog:** explain the documented flavor, float inspiration and variant distinctions in a short educational narrative (PROD-001 to PROD-004). Example tactic: a “what is Cherry Float?” article outline with claim IDs attached to factual passages; no invented consumer testimonials or launch history.
- **Advertising:** test concise flavor-led wording as a proposed creative approach, keeping variant identity clear. Example tactic: alternative headlines built from the same three pillars; no superiority, ingredient, health or scarcity assertions. Brand voice and tone: playful, warm, clear and lightly nostalgic. Use everyday flavor language and short sentences. Keep LinkedIn informative, email focused, the blog explanatory and ads concise. Avoid exaggerated taste promises, unsupported health language, fabricated testimonials and nostalgia claims presented as measured consumer reactions.

### Key Launch Messages

- **Coca-Cola flavor exploration:** explain the documented cherry and vanilla notes within the Coca-Cola identity (PROD-001, PROD-002). Use clear flavor language; “vanilla notes” is not an ingredient claim.
- **Classic float inspiration:** make this familiar reference the central storytelling theme (PROD-003). Test whether this story resonates; do not imply dairy content or validated consumer preference.
- **Clear variant choice:** distinguish the documented original/full-sugar and zero-sugar variants as product information, not a unique competitive advantage (PROD-004). Mention a variant as available only when evidence and both scenario sources support the selected market/date; otherwise withhold availability language.
